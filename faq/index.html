Tax Questions Kansas City Freelancers and Families Ask Most

Short, plain answers to the questions that come up most for freelancers, gig workers and families in Kansas City and Lawrence, each with a guide that goes deeper.

By Robert Allen Bautista · Kansas City tax educator · Published 2026-11-24

These answers are general information for education. Rules and amounts change each year, so use the linked guides and IRS.gov to confirm the current details.

Filing and refunds

How do I know the IRS accepted my e-filed return?

The IRS says an electronically filed return is not considered filed until the IRS acknowledges acceptance of its electronic part, and a rejected return is treated as not filed. Ask your preparer or software for the acceptance acknowledgment. You can also check Where's My Refund, which shows status 24 hours after you e-file a current-year return, or your IRS Online Account. Read more: Where Is My Tax Refund? How Long It Takes and Why It Gets Delayed

How long does a tax refund take?

The IRS says most e-filed refunds arrive in about 3 weeks (21 days). Refund status is available 24 hours after you e-file a current-year return and 4 weeks after you file a paper return. For a mailed return, the IRS says to allow 6 or more weeks. Read more: Where Is My Tax Refund? How Long It Takes and Why It Gets Delayed

What should I check on my return before I sign it?

Read it first and ask about anything unclear. The IRS says never to sign a blank or incomplete return, and to check the routing and account numbers on the return before you sign. Your refund should go to you, not into the preparer's bank account. Read more: What a Tax Preparer Checks Before Signing Your Return

What do I do if a W-2 is missing?

Check your IRS online account, then ask your employer. If you still do not have it by the end of February, the IRS says you can call 800-829-1040, and it will contact the employer and send you Form 4852. If you must file before the form arrives, you can estimate your wages and withholding on Form 4852. Read more: Missing W-2 or 1099 in Late January: Form 4852

How do I fix a mistake after I file?

The IRS says that if a form arrives later and differs from the figures you used, you file Form 1040-X to amend your return. Keep a copy of what you filed and of the correct form. Read more: Amended Return (Form 1040-X): When and How to Fix a Mistake

IRS letters, penalties and payments

The IRS says it has no record of my return. What does that mean?

This is usually a CP59, CP515 or CP516 notice. The IRS says that if you filed within the last 8 weeks you generally do not need to act. Otherwise, check that the name, Social Security number and tax year match your return and that the return was not rejected, then follow the steps on the notice. Read more: Decoding an IRS Notice: What the Number in the Corner Tells You

What are the penalties for filing or paying late?

The IRS failure-to-file penalty is 5% of the unpaid tax for each month or part of a month, up to 25%. The failure-to-pay penalty is 0.5% per month, up to 25%. When both apply in the same month, the filing penalty is reduced by the payment penalty. Read more: IRS Payment Plans Explained

What if I cannot pay what I owe?

The IRS says to file on time and pay what you can, then ask about a payment plan. A short-term plan gives you 180 days or less, and individuals who owe less than $100,000 in combined tax, penalties and interest can apply online. Long-term plans have setup fees that vary by how you apply. Read more: IRS Payment Plans Explained

How do I stop someone else from filing a return with my Social Security number?

The IRS offers an Identity Protection PIN, a six-digit number that helps prevent someone else from filing using your SSN or ITIN. Anyone who can verify their identity can get one, and a new PIN is generated each year. Read more: Identity Protection PIN: Stop Someone Else Filing Your Return

Choosing a preparer or software

How do I check whether a tax preparer is legitimate?

The IRS says anyone can be a paid preparer as long as they have a PTIN, so a PTIN alone is not a credential. A paid preparer must sign your return and include the PTIN. The IRS also offers a Directory of Federal Tax Return Preparers you can use to look up credentials. Read more: Ghost Tax Preparers: Red Flags and How to Check a PTIN

If my preparer makes a mistake, who is responsible?

The IRS says that no matter who prepares it, the accuracy of a tax return is ultimately the taxpayer's responsibility. You can ask the IRS to remove penalties for reasonable cause, and you can report a preparer with Form 14157. Read more: What a Tax Preparer Checks Before Signing Your Return

I paid through my refund. Why has the money not arrived?

The IRS says refunds taken as an advance or refund transfer are deposited into a financial institution set up by your preparer or software provider. Contact your provider, preparer or that institution. The IRS says not to file Form 3911 to trace these. Read more: Where Is My Tax Refund? How Long It Takes and Why It Gets Delayed

Free help in Kansas City and Lawrence

Where can I get free tax help near Kansas City?

Use the IRS VITA/TCE locator or call 800-906-9887. The IRS says VITA sites generally help people who make about $70,244 or less, people with disabilities and limited English-speaking taxpayers. Services and hours vary by site. IRS Free File guided software has an income limit, and the IRS site currently says Free File has closed and to check back in January 2027. Read more: Tax Season Document Checklist: What to Gather Before You File

Is there free tax help in Lawrence, Kansas?

Enter a Lawrence ZIP code in the IRS VITA/TCE locator to see current sites and hours. Sites vary by season, so confirm before you go. For people 60 and older, TCE sites, most run by AARP Tax-Aide, can be reached at 888-227-7669 from January through April. Read more: Tax Season Document Checklist: What to Gather Before You File

I am several years behind on filing. Where do I start?

You can request a wage and income transcript, which shows W-2s and 1099s reported to the IRS for the current and nine prior years. The IRS accepts e-filed returns for the two most recent prior years through an authorized e-file provider. If you owe and cannot pay, file anyway and ask about a payment plan.
