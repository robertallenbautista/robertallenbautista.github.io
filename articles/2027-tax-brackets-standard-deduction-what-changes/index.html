2027 Tax Brackets and Standard Deduction: What Changes for Your 2027 Paycheck

The IRS published the 2027 numbers on November 5, 2026. Here is what they are and what to do with them.

By Robert Allen Bautista · Kansas City tax educator · Published 2026-11-25

Key takeaways

  • According to IRS Revenue Procedure 2026-35, the 2027 standard deduction is $33,200 for married couples filing jointly, $24,925 for heads of household, and $16,600 for single filers and married people filing separately.
  • The seven tax rates still run from 10% to 37%. The income thresholds where each rate starts are what the IRS adjusted.
  • These amounts apply to your 2027 return, which you file in 2028. The return you file this spring uses the 2026 figures.
  • The best time to check your withholding is the first pay stub of January, and again after any raise or life change.

On November 5, 2026, the IRS released Revenue Procedure 2026-35, which lists inflation-adjusted amounts for tax year 2027. The numbers matter now for one practical reason: your withholding, your estimated payments and your retirement contributions all restart on January 1. This article walks through the amounts that affect most households.

One timing point first. These are 2027 figures, so they apply to the return you file in 2028. The return you file this spring covers 2026 and uses 2026 amounts.

The 2027 standard deduction

The standard deduction is the amount the IRS subtracts from your income before applying the tax rates, unless you itemize. For taxable years beginning in 2027, Revenue Procedure 2026-35 lists:

  • Married individuals filing joint returns and surviving spouses: $33,200.
  • Heads of households: $24,925.
  • Unmarried individuals who are not surviving spouses or heads of households: $16,600.
  • Married individuals filing separate returns: $16,600.

Two smaller rules sit in the same section. The additional standard deduction for people who are 65 or older or blind is $1,700, and it rises to $2,100 if you are also unmarried and not a surviving spouse. A person who can be claimed as a dependent on someone else's return is limited to the greater of $1,400 or $500 plus their earned income. If you are not sure whether to take the standard deduction at all, my guide to standard deduction or itemizing has a simple test.

The 2027 tax brackets

The rates stay at 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35% and 37%. What changes is the taxable income at which each rate begins. Taxable income means your income after the standard deduction or itemized deductions. These thresholds come from the rate tables in Revenue Procedure 2026-35.

Single

  • 10% on taxable income up to $12,800.
  • 12% from $12,800 to $52,025.
  • 22% from $52,025 to $109,125.
  • 24% from $109,125 to $208,325.
  • 32% from $208,325 to $264,550.
  • 35% from $264,550 to $661,375.
  • 37% above $661,375.

Married filing jointly

  • 10% on taxable income up to $25,600.
  • 12% from $25,600 to $104,050.
  • 22% from $104,050 to $218,250.
  • 24% from $218,250 to $416,650.
  • 32% from $416,650 to $529,100.
  • 35% from $529,100 to $793,650.
  • 37% above $793,650.

Head of household

  • 10% on taxable income up to $18,250.
  • 12% from $18,250 to $69,650.
  • 22% from $69,650 to $109,100.
  • 24% from $109,100 to $208,300.
  • 32% from $208,300 to $264,550.
  • 35% from $264,550 to $661,350.
  • 37% above $661,350.

Married filing separately uses the same thresholds as single filers up to the 35% bracket. The 35% bracket ends at $396,825 for married people filing separately, and 37% applies above that.

A worked example

This is a fictional example with made-up numbers. Say a single filer earns $60,000 in wages in 2027 and has no other income, adjustments or credits. The $16,600 standard deduction leaves $43,400 of taxable income.

The 2027 table puts the tax on the first $12,800 at $1,280, then 12% on the amount above that. The $30,600 above $12,800 is taxed at 12%, which is $3,672. The total is $4,952. None of that income is taxed at 22%, even though 22% is the next step up.

People miss this all the time. Brackets are marginal, so a raise that pushes you into a higher bracket taxes only the dollars above the line at the higher rate.

The child tax credit

The IRS release says the maximum child tax credit for 2027 is $2,300, with a refundable portion of up to $1,800. A credit is subtracted from the tax itself, so it is worth more than a deduction of the same size. Eligibility rules, such as the child's age and your income, still apply, so confirm them before you plan around it.

What to do before January

Your employer's payroll system calculates withholding from the Form W-4 you gave them, so new IRS figures do not change your paycheck on their own. A short checklist:

  • Look at your first January pay stub and compare the federal withholding with last year's.
  • Run the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator if your income, filing status or number of jobs changed. My article on a paycheck withholding checkup shows how.
  • If you have freelance income on top of a job, plan for payments of your own. See the 2027 estimated tax dates.
  • Read the 2027 contribution limits before you set your 401(k) percentage for the year.

The revenue procedure also says that if Congress amends the tax code for 2027 after the release date, taxpayers should look for additional guidance. These are the IRS's current numbers, and the law can still change.

Questions to ask your preparer

  • Will I likely take the standard deduction or itemize in 2027?
  • Does my withholding match my expected tax, or should I adjust my W-4?
  • If I have side income, how much should I set aside each quarter?
  • Do I qualify for the child tax credit next year?

Frequently asked questions

What is the standard deduction for 2027?

According to IRS Revenue Procedure 2026-35, the 2027 standard deduction is $33,200 for married couples filing jointly, $24,925 for heads of household, and $16,600 for single filers and married people filing separately.

Do the tax rates change in 2027?

The rates stay at 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35% and 37%. The IRS adjusted the taxable income thresholds where each rate starts, for example $12,800 for the top of the 10% bracket for single filers.

Do the 2027 numbers apply to the return I file this spring?

No. The 2027 amounts apply to your 2027 return, which you file in 2028. The return you file this spring covers 2026 and uses the 2026 figures.
