Missing a W-2 or 1099 in Late January? Who to Call and When Form 4852 Helps

A form that has not arrived can be fixed, and the order of the steps matters.

By Robert Allen Bautista · Kansas City tax preparer and accountant · Published 2026-11-23

Key takeaways

  • Employers have to get your W-2 to you by January 31. In 2027 that date is a Sunday, so the practical due date is Monday, February 1, 2027.
  • Check your IRS online account first, then the employer's payroll portal, your spam folder and your old mailing address.
  • If you contacted the payer and still have nothing by the end of February, the IRS says to call. It will contact the payer and send you Form 4852.
  • Form 4852 substitutes for a missing W-2 or 1099-R using your best estimate, but the IRS says it cannot determine the deductible amounts for qualified tips or overtime.

Every year a few people reach the end of January with a stack of tax forms and one obvious hole. The W-2 from the last job never showed up. The 1099 from a client is nowhere to be found. You can fix this, and the order of steps matters.

This article walks through that order for the 2026 tax year, the return you will file in 2027.

Know the date you are waiting on

Employers have to get your W-2 to you by January 31. In 2027 that date lands on a Sunday, so the practical due date moves to Monday, February 1, 2027. The IRS tax topic on missing forms uses the same date: if your Form W-2 or 1099-R is not available by February 1, 2027, or the information on it is wrong, start by checking your online account with the IRS.

So a form that has not arrived on January 29 is not late yet. Give it until Monday, February 1 before you start making calls.

Step 1: Look in the places the form may already be

Before you contact anyone, check these spots.

  • Your spam folder and the email address you gave the payer. Many employers and payers now deliver forms through a portal and send only a notice.
  • The employer's payroll portal. Many payroll companies post the W-2 in your account and never mail a paper copy unless you ask.
  • Your old mailing address. If you moved during 2026, the form may have gone to the place you left.
  • Your IRS online account. The IRS says to check there first for the information it has on file.

If the form is in one of those places, you are done. Download it, save a copy, and go on with your return.

Step 2: Contact the employer or payer

If the form is not in your online account, the IRS says to contact the employer or payer that issued it. Be specific when you ask. Say which tax year you need, give the exact name on the form, and tell them the address where you want it sent or the portal where you want it posted.

Confirm that they have your current address. If you moved, give it to them in writing, even if that is just an email, and keep a copy. Ask when they sent the form and how. If they tell you it was mailed to the wrong address, ask them to reissue it.

Write down the date of every contact and who you spoke with. You may need that note later.

This applies to freelancers too. A client who paid you enough during the year may owe you a Form 1099-NEC, and you can ask for it the same way. Ask your preparer what the reporting threshold is for 2026 payments. Even if you never get a form, the income is still taxable and still belongs on your return. The amount you earned does not depend on whether a 1099 exists.

Step 3: Wait until the end of February, then call the IRS

If you have contacted the payer and the form still has not arrived, the IRS tax topic says to call the agency if you still do not have it by the end of February. Have these items ready when you call:

  • Your name, address with ZIP code, birth date and taxpayer identification number.
  • The employer or payer's name, address with ZIP code, and EIN if you have it.
  • A rough idea of your wages and withholding, which you can estimate from your last pay stub.

The IRS will contact the employer or payer on your behalf. It will also send you a Form 4852 along with instructions.

What Form 4852 is

Form 4852 is a substitute for Form W-2 or Form 1099-R. You fill it out with your best estimate of wages, payments and withholding for the year. You can then file your return with it if the real form is not going to arrive in time.

Where do you get a good estimate? Your final pay stub of the year usually shows year-to-date wages and year-to-date federal withholding. Bank deposits can fill in gaps. If a payer sent a final statement or a year-end earnings summary, use that.

Take care with the numbers. Overstating your withholding or understating your wages can lead to a notice later.

The tips and overtime limit

For 2026 returns, some workers may be claiming the new deductions for qualified tips or qualified overtime. The IRS notes that Form 4852 cannot determine those deductible amounts for you. Those figures come from the employer's reporting, so a missing W-2 can slow that deduction down. If you work in a tipped job, ask the employer to reissue the W-2 instead of relying on an estimate. If you already have tip income that you never reported to your employer, the IRS points to Form 4137 for that.

If the real form shows up after you file

Sometimes you file with Form 4852 and the actual form arrives a week later. Compare the numbers. If they match what you estimated, you do not need to do anything. If they differ, file Form 1040-X to amend your return with the correct figures.

An amended return takes extra time, and it is easier to file once than twice. That is the main reason to try hard to get the real form first.

Should you wait or file?

Waiting a few days or even a couple of weeks is often fine. Refunds are not paid until the IRS processes your return, and filing before you have all your forms gets you nothing extra. Filing with a missing form that you intend to fix later can create a second round of paperwork.

On the other hand, if you are owed a large refund or need to file for a loan, a rental application or a financial aid form, an estimate on Form 4852 may be worth it. Talk it through with a preparer who can look at your final pay stub and tell you how close your estimate is likely to be.

What to bring to your preparer

If you are meeting with a preparer, bring your last pay stub of 2026, any online account printouts, a note of every call you made, and the name of the employer or payer. If you have a bank deposit record showing the income, bring that too.

Questions to ask your preparer

  • Is it better to wait for the real form or file with an estimate on Form 4852 in my case?
  • How close does my final pay stub put me to what the W-2 will show?
  • If I file with an estimate and the real form differs, will I need to amend?
  • Does my missing form affect any tips or overtime deduction I plan to claim?
  • If I earned freelance income but never got a 1099, how should I report it?

Sources
