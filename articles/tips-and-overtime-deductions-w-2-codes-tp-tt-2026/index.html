The New Tips and Overtime Deductions on Your 2026 Return: Who Qualifies and What to Ask HR

Both deductions are real, and in 2026 your W-2 is where the numbers should show up.

By Robert Allen Bautista · Kansas City tax educator · Published 2026-11-24

Key takeaways

  • The IRS says the deduction for qualified tips is up to $25,000 per year, and the overtime deduction is up to $12,500 per year, or $25,000 on a joint return.
  • Both phase out when modified adjusted gross income is over $150,000, or over $300,000 for joint filers. Married people must file jointly, and you must have a valid Social Security number.
  • For 2026, the IRS says employers must report qualified overtime in box 12 of Form W-2 with code TT, and tips in box 12 with code TP, with the occupation code in box 14b.
  • Form 4852 cannot determine the deductible amounts for qualified tips or overtime, so a wrong or missing W-2 needs a corrected Form W-2c.

Congress added two new deductions in 2025 for workers who earn tips or overtime. Both appear on the federal return, and you can claim either one whether you itemize or take the standard deduction. For 2026, most workers will see the largest change on the W-2 their employer sends.

This article covers who qualifies, the dollar limits, how the W-2 reporting works for 2026, and the questions to ask your payroll or HR department before January.

The tips deduction

The IRS says the deduction for qualified tips is up to $25,000 per year. It applies to employees and to self-employed people who received qualified tips. Qualified tips are voluntary cash or charged tips from customers, including shared tips, in occupations that customarily and regularly received tips on or before December 31, 2024. The IRS publishes a list of those occupations, so check that list if you are unsure whether your job counts.

Several conditions apply.

  • If you are married, you must file jointly to claim the deduction.
  • You must have a valid Social Security number.
  • If you are self-employed, the deduction cannot be more than your net income from the business in which you earned the tips, figured before the deduction.
  • The deduction begins to phase out when modified adjusted gross income is over $150,000, or over $300,000 for joint filers.

The overtime deduction

The overtime deduction is up to $12,500 per year, or $25,000 for a joint return. The IRS says the deduction is for the pay that exceeds your regular rate of pay. The same phaseout applies: it starts when modified adjusted gross income is over $150,000, or over $300,000 for joint filers. As with tips, married people must file jointly and you must have a valid Social Security number.

The IRS guidance explains that qualified overtime is compensation paid under section 7 of the Fair Labor Standards Act that is above your regular rate. In practice, the deduction generally covers only the extra half in time and a half. In a made-up example, if you earn $20 an hour and are paid $30 for an overtime hour, the part that counts is the $10 above your regular rate. Overtime pay that your employer owes under another rule or contract, and not under that federal law, may not count. Ask your preparer if your pay arrangement is unusual.

What changes on the W-2 for 2026

For 2025, the IRS gave employers transition relief. Many employees did not see a separate entry for qualified overtime on their 2025 W-2, even though they earned it, and the IRS pointed people to the Schedule 1-A instructions to work out the amount.

That relief does not carry into 2026. The IRS says employers must report qualified overtime in box 12 of Form W-2 using code TT, and only the "half" portion of time and a half is reported there. For tips, the 2026 W-2 reports total cash tips in box 12 using code TP, and box 14b should show the Treasury Tipped Occupation Code for the job in which you earned the tips.

If an employer finds an error in code TT, it must file a corrected Form W-2c and give you a copy. The same step is the fix if your W-2 shows the wrong amount.

So the 2026 W-2 you get in early 2027 should carry the numbers for both deductions. Review them when it arrives.

Check these on your W-2 when it arrives

  • Box 12, code TT, for qualified overtime compensation.
  • Box 12, code TP, for qualified tips, and box 14b for the occupation code.
  • Whether the code TT amount matches only the extra half of your overtime premium rather than your full overtime pay.
  • Whether the tip total roughly matches the tips you reported to your employer during the year.

Tips that you never reported to your employer follow a separate process. The IRS points to Form 4137 to report unreported tip income. Also, if you are waiting on a missing W-2 and thinking of using Form 4852, the IRS says that form cannot determine the deductible amounts for qualified overtime or tips.

A made-up example

This is a made-up example. A server is married and files jointly. Her W-2 for 2026 shows $9,000 in box 12 with code TP, and her box 14b has the code for her job. Her household income is below the phaseout level. She may be able to deduct the $9,000 on top of the standard deduction, subject to the rules for her situation. A second made-up example: a warehouse worker's W-2 shows $4,000 in box 12 with code TT. That amount is the premium half of his overtime, and it is below the $12,500 limit for a single filer, so the full amount may be deductible.

Neither example reflects any real person. They show where the numbers come from.

What to ask HR or payroll before January

You do not need to wait for the W-2 to learn how your employer is set up. In December, you can ask.

  • Will my 2026 W-2 include box 12 codes TT or TP?
  • How does payroll decide which of my pay counts as qualified overtime?
  • How are tips reported to you, and will they be broken out the way the W-2 requires?
  • Will the W-2 show the occupation code in box 14b?
  • If there is an error, how fast can payroll issue a corrected W-2c?

Employers use different payroll systems, and some will have set these up better than others. A short email in December is easier than a correction in March.

Keep your pay stubs

Keep your last pay stubs of the year. The year-to-date lines can show total overtime hours, overtime pay and reported tips. If something on the W-2 looks off, those stubs help your preparer sort out what the right number should be.

Questions to ask your preparer

  • Do my tips or overtime qualify under the IRS definitions?
  • Does my income put me in the phaseout range?
  • Do the W-2 amounts in codes TT and TP match my pay records?
  • If I am self-employed, how do the tips deduction and my net income interact?
  • What should I do if my employer's W-2 is wrong?
