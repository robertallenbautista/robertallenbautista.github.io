2027 Contribution Limits: 401(k), IRA, HSA and the Social Security Wage Base

The 2027 limits are all official now. Here they are in one list, with where each came from.

By Robert Allen Bautista · Kansas City tax preparer and accountant · Published 2026-11-25

Key takeaways

  • The 2027 401(k) employee limit is $25,500, according to the IRS announcement of November 12, 2026. The IRA limit stays at $7,500.
  • The 2027 HSA limits are $4,500 for self-only coverage and $9,000 for family coverage, per IRS Revenue Procedure 2026-24.
  • The Social Security wage base for 2027 is $192,300, according to the Social Security Administration.
  • Each figure below comes from an official IRS or SSA publication. Anything this article leaves out is listed near the end.

Different agencies publish the yearly limits at different times, so the numbers end up scattered. The HSA limits came out first, in a revenue procedure released around the end of May 2026. The Social Security wage base followed in October, and the IRS retirement plan limits arrived on November 12, 2026. All three are now official. This article puts them in one place and says where each one comes from.

These limits apply to 2027 contributions, so you can set your payroll percentages in January with the right target in mind. If you are still finishing 2026, the 2026 limits apply until December 31.

401(k) and similar plans

The IRS announcement says the amount individuals can contribute to their 401(k) plans in 2027 has increased to $25,500, up from $24,500 for 2026. The announcement and Notice 2026-66 also cover other employer plans, so check the notice if you have a 403(b) or a government plan.

  • The catch-up contribution for people 50 and over increases to $8,500, up from $8,000 for 2026.
  • For ages 60 to 63, the IRS says the higher catch-up limit is $11,750 instead of the $8,500.

Whether your plan offers every option is up to your employer, so read your plan documents or ask HR.

IRAs

  • The limit on annual contributions to an Individual Retirement Account remains $7,500.
  • The IRA catch-up contribution limit for people 50 and over remains $1,100.

The Roth IRA income phase-out ranges rose for 2027. The IRS says the range is between $158,000 and $173,000 for singles and heads of household, and between $250,000 and $260,000 for married couples filing jointly. Inside those ranges, the amount you can put into a Roth IRA shrinks as your income rises.

SIMPLE plans

If you or your employer has a SIMPLE plan, the standard limit rises to $17,500 for 2027, up from $17,000. The IRS also lists a higher amount of $19,100, up from $18,100, that applies in certain cases. The SIMPLE catch-up stays at $4,000.

Health savings accounts

Revenue Procedure 2026-24 sets the 2027 HSA numbers:

  • The annual contribution limit is $4,500 with self-only coverage under a high deductible health plan and $9,000 with family coverage.
  • A high deductible health plan must have an annual deductible of at least $1,750 for self-only coverage or $3,500 for family coverage.
  • Out-of-pocket expenses, not counting premiums, cannot exceed $8,700 for self-only coverage or $17,400 for family coverage.

The extra $1,000 catch-up for people 55 and older is set by law and does not adjust for inflation. My earlier guide to HSA contribution limits and the tax savings explains how the deduction works.

The Social Security wage base

The Social Security Administration announced that in 2027, individual taxable earnings of up to $192,300 will be subject to Social Security tax, up from $184,500 in 2026. Earnings above that amount do not owe the Social Security part of payroll tax. The 6.2% employee share at the cap works out to $11,922.60, and an employer pays a matching amount.

If you are self-employed, you pay both halves, so the cap matters most in your strong years. Medicare tax has no wage cap. My article on why a first freelance tax bill is so big shows how self-employment tax adds up.

A fictional example

This is a made-up example. A married couple earns $140,000 combined and wants to save more in 2027. One spouse has a 401(k), and the family has an HSA-eligible health plan with family coverage. They could put up to $25,500 into the 401(k), up to $9,000 into the family HSA and up to $7,500 each into IRAs, subject to the income and participation rules. Few households max out every account, and they do not need to. The limits are ceilings, and the right amount depends on cash flow and goals.

What this article leaves out

I did not list the income ranges that limit deductible traditional IRA contributions, the Saver's Credit tiers other than the joint-filer figure of $83,000, or the Section 415 plan limits. Check IRS Notice 2026-66 for the full set. The IRS may also publish updated guidance later, so check the sources below before you act on any single number.
