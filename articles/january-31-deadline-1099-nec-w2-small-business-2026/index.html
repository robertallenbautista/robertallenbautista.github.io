The January 31 Deadline for Small Business Owners: 1099-NEC, W-2 and the New $2,000 Threshold

Paid a contractor or an employee in 2026? Here is what is due, when it is due, and what changed this year.

By Robert Allen Bautista · Kansas City tax educator · Published 2026-11-22

Key takeaways

  • January 31, 2027 falls on a Sunday. The IRS says a due date on a weekend or legal holiday moves to the next business day, so Form 1099-NEC is due Monday, February 1, 2027. The 2026 W-2 instructions list February 1, 2027 for Copy A and for employee copies.
  • For tax years beginning after 2025, the reporting threshold for Forms 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC is $2,000, up from $600. The IRS says it may be adjusted for inflation beginning in 2027.
  • Collect a Form W-9 from each contractor before paying them, and catch up on missing ones in December.
  • Payments by credit card or through a third party network are reported on Form 1099-K by the processor, so they stay off your 1099-NEC.
  • Late penalties for returns due in 2027 start at $60 per return.

Every January, small business owners learn that paying someone creates paperwork. If you paid contractors or employees during 2026, forms are due right at the start of 2027, and both the date and the dollar threshold shifted this year. Here is how I would work through it, in order.

The real due date: February 1, 2027

The Form 1099-NEC instructions say you must file on or before January 31, and that when the date lands on a weekend or legal holiday, the due date is the next business day. January 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so the practical deadline is Monday, February 1, 2027. The 2026 General Instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3 state February 1, 2027 for mailing or e-filing Copy A with the Social Security Administration. Employee copies are generally due the same day.

Who gets which form

Contractors get Form 1099-NEC

You file Form 1099-NEC for each person you paid, in the course of your business during the year, at least $2,000 in nonemployee compensation. That covers freelancers, consultants and other people who do work for you without being on your payroll. Payments to a corporation, including an LLC treated as a C or S corporation, are generally exempt. Legal services are the exception: the corporation exemption does not apply to payments for legal services, and attorney fees of $2,000 or more paid in the course of your business are reportable.

Employees get Form W-2

For people on your payroll, you furnish Form W-2 and send Copy A to the Social Security Administration with a Form W-3, which the IRS describes as the form used to transmit Copy A of Forms W-2. Whether a worker is an employee or a contractor depends on the facts of the relationship, so I would rather review that question with you in the fall than argue about it in January.

The threshold change for 2026 payments

The IRS instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC state that for tax years beginning after 2025, the minimum threshold for reporting certain payments increased to $2,000, and that it may be adjusted for inflation beginning in calendar year 2027. That applies to both forms. A few payment types keep the old figure. Gross proceeds paid to attorneys, reported in Form 1099-MISC box 10, is one example the IRS lists at $600.

A contractor you paid $1,800 in 2026 no longer needs a 1099-NEC from you. Still, a vendor at $1,500 in March can be at $2,500 by December, so keep collecting W-9s.

Collect the W-9 in December

You cannot report a payment well without the payee's name and taxpayer identification number (TIN). Form W-9 is how a payee gives you a correct TIN, and the IRS notes you may use it to obtain the TIN for an attorney you pay.

A December checklist:

  • List everyone you paid for services in 2026 by check, cash, bank transfer or app.
  • Add up each person's total and mark the ones at or above $2,000.
  • Match the list against the W-9s in your file, and request the missing ones now.

What counts as reportable, and what does not

Reportable payments are generally for services, paid to a non-employee in the course of your business, and at or above the threshold. Payments for personal reasons fall outside the form. The big exclusion for most small businesses is the way you paid. The IRS states that payments made by credit card or through a third party network are not subject to reporting on Form 1099-MISC or Form 1099-NEC. They are reported on Form 1099-K by the payment settlement entity.

That matters when you pay a contractor with a card or an online payment platform. The processor handles the reporting for that money, and you leave it off your 1099-NEC. A third party network files a 1099-K only when its reporting test is met, which the 1099-K instructions describe as gross payments over $20,000 and more than 200 transactions in the calendar year.

A made-up example

This example is made up to show the steps. Maple Street Print Shop is a fictional sole proprietorship in Kansas City. In 2026 it paid a freelance designer $3,400 by bank transfer and a photographer $1,800 by check. A delivery driver received $2,600 through a payment app that is a third party network, and a web developer received $5,000 on the shop's credit card.

  • The designer's $3,400 is at or above $2,000, so the shop files a 1099-NEC.
  • The photographer's $1,800 is under $2,000, so no 1099-NEC is required.
  • The driver's $2,600 went through a third party network, so any reporting is a 1099-K from the network, and the shop files no 1099-NEC for it.
  • The web developer's $5,000 was a card payment, so any reporting is a 1099-K from the card processor.

The shop files one 1099-NEC, for the designer, using the W-9 it collected in December. Had it filed that one form within 30 days after the due date, the penalty would be $60.

E-filing and IRIS

The e-file threshold is 10 returns. The IRS lowered it for returns required to be filed on or after January 1, 2024, and the count adds up all of your information returns, so W-2s and 1099s together can push you over. If you file 10 or more, you must file electronically. The IRS Information Returns Intake System, called IRIS, handles Forms 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC. Through the IRIS Taxpayer Portal you can e-file up to 100 returns at a time, by manual entry or a .csv upload. You need an IRIS Transmitter Control Code (TCC), which the IRS says can only be used for IRIS.

Penalties for filing late

The IRS lists these amounts per return for returns and statements due in 2027, which is tax year 2026:

  • Up to 30 days late: $60 per return.
  • 31 days late through August 1: $130 per return.
  • After August 1 or not filed: $340 per return.
  • Intentional disregard: $690 per return, with no maximum.

The penalty applies separately to each late return and each payee statement, and each tier has an annual cap. If you are late, file as soon as you can, because the amount rises with the delay.

Questions to ask your tax preparer

  • Which of my 2026 payees reach the $2,000 threshold, and which do not?
  • Do I have a current W-9 for everyone who does?
  • Did any of my payments go through a card or payment app, and does that change which forms apply?
  • Do I have 10 or more information returns in total, and do I need an IRIS TCC?
  • Are any of my contractors really employees, and should I look at that before next year?
  • What is my plan if I miss the February 1 date?

Frequently asked questions

Is the 1099-NEC deadline January 31 or February 1, 2027?

January 31, 2027 is a Sunday. The IRS instructions say that when the due date falls on a weekend or legal holiday, it is the next business day, which makes it Monday, February 1, 2027.

What is the 1099-NEC threshold for 2026 payments?

$2,000 for tax years beginning after 2025, up from $600. The IRS says it may be adjusted for inflation beginning in calendar year 2027.

Do payments by credit card go on a 1099-NEC?

No. The IRS states that card and third party network payments are reported on Form 1099-K by the payment settlement entity, so they are not reported on Form 1099-NEC or 1099-MISC.
