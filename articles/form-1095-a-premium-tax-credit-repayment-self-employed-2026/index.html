Form 1095-A and the Premium Tax Credit for Self-Employed and Gig Workers in 2026

If your income moves around, so can the settlement on your health insurance credit.

By Robert Allen Bautista · Kansas City tax educator, CPA candidate · Published 2026-11-24

Key takeaways

  • Form 1095-A comes from the Marketplace. Form 8962 is the one you file with your Form 1040, and the IRS says you use the 1095-A information to complete it.
  • Form 8962 compares the advance credit paid on your behalf with the credit you qualify for based on final household income and family size.
  • The IRS says repayment limits are removed beginning with tax years after 2025, so for 2026 an excess advance credit is generally repaid in full.
  • Wait for your Form 1095-A before you finish the return, and update your Marketplace income estimate during the year if your income changes.

If you bought health insurance through the Health Insurance Marketplace and you get monthly help paying the premium, you will file a form with your return that many people have never heard of. That form is Form 8962, and it settles up the help you got during the year against the help you actually qualified for. If you are self-employed or do gig work, your income moves around, and this settlement can swing a refund or a bill.

A major repayment rule changed starting with the 2026 tax year. Read this before you file.

The two forms involved

Form 1095-A, Health Insurance Marketplace Statement, comes from the Marketplace. It lists the months you were covered, the premium for the plan, the benchmark plan premium used in the credit math, and the advance payments of the premium tax credit sent to your insurer each month.

Form 8962, Premium Tax Credit, is the one you file with your Form 1040. The IRS says you use the information on Form 1095-A to complete Form 8962. The form compares the advance credit paid on your behalf with the credit you are entitled to based on your final household income and family size for the year.

If the advance credit was less than your actual credit, you get the difference as an added credit. If the advance credit was more than your actual credit, you owe the difference.

Why income drives the credit

The credit depends on household income. When you signed up, you estimated your income for the year. The Marketplace used that estimate to set your monthly advance payment.

For a W-2 employee, that estimate is usually close. A freelancer, rideshare driver or small business owner can miss by a wide margin. A slow summer and a strong fall can leave you with a final income very different from your guess. If your final income is higher than your estimate, the advance payments may turn out to be too large. If it is lower, you may be owed more credit.

If you are self-employed, the income used for this form comes from the return itself. Net profit from Schedule C, after expenses, flows into your adjusted gross income. So your business expenses and your self-employment records affect your health coverage credit too.

What changed for 2026

Before 2026, there were limits on how much of an excess advance payment you had to pay back, and the limits depended on your income as a percentage of the poverty line. The IRS says those repayment limits are removed beginning with tax years after 2025. Under the IRS questions and answers on the premium tax credit, there is no repayment cap for tax years after 2025, and the excess is added to your total tax. That reduces a refund or increases a balance due.

Put simply, if you received too much advance credit for 2026, you generally repay all of the excess when you file in 2027.

The draft 2026 instructions for Form 8962 also say that, for tax years beginning after 2025, a household with income above 400% of the federal poverty line for its family size is not eligible for the premium tax credit. These are draft instructions, so confirm the final version before you rely on a specific line. Crossing that income level can cost you more than it used to.

A made-up example

Here is a made-up example, with round numbers. Suppose a freelance designer estimates her 2026 income low in November 2025 and receives advance payments all year. In the spring of 2027, her final income turns out higher than the estimate. Form 8962 recalculates her credit using the final number and finds she received more advance credit than she qualified for. For a 2026 return, the full difference is added to her tax. In earlier years a cap might have limited that bill. For 2026, it does not.

The numbers here are invented to show the process. Your own result depends on your income, family size, location and plan.

What to do before you file

Wait for your Form 1095-A before you finish the return. Do not file using the amounts from an online account or a premium statement from the insurance company. The IRS expects you to use the Marketplace form.

When it arrives, check it. Make sure the months of coverage are right, the people listed are the people who were on your plan, and the advance payment amounts match what you remember. If anything looks wrong, contact the Marketplace and ask for a corrected form.

If you got a second 1095-A because you changed plans or moved, you will have more than one form to enter.

Steps to take during the year

If your income changes during the year, go back to the Marketplace and update your estimate. That is the easiest way to avoid a surprise at tax time. If you expect a big raise in business profit, a larger estimate lowers your advance payments, and the final settlement is smaller. If your income falls, a lower estimate can raise your advance payments.

Keep a simple running total of net profit, even if you only update it each quarter. Freelancers who already track income for estimated taxes can use the same numbers.

Questions to ask your preparer

  • Does the income I reported to the Marketplace match my actual 2026 net profit?
  • Do I owe back any excess advance payments, and how much?
  • Did I cross the income level that ends credit eligibility for 2026?
  • Are all months and family members on my Form 1095-A correct?
  • Should I change my Marketplace income estimate now for 2027?

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Form 1095-A and Form 8962?

Form 1095-A, Health Insurance Marketplace Statement, comes from the Marketplace and lists your months of coverage, the plan premium, the benchmark plan premium and the advance payments. Form 8962, Premium Tax Credit, is the one you file with your Form 1040, using the information on Form 1095-A.

Is there a repayment cap on excess advance premium tax credit for 2026?

No. The IRS says the repayment limits are removed beginning with tax years after 2025, and the excess is added to your total tax. That reduces a refund or increases a balance due.

Should I file before my Form 1095-A arrives?

No. Wait for your Form 1095-A. The IRS expects you to use the Marketplace form, not the amounts from an online account or a premium statement from the insurance company.
