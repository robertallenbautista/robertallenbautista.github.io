Made a Mistake on Your Tax Return? When and How to File Form 1040-X

You found a missing 1099 or a wrong deduction after you filed. Here is when an amended return is the right fix, when to wait, and how the process works.

By Robert Allen Bautista · Kansas City tax educator · Published 2026-11-16

Key takeaways

  • Use Form 1040-X to change a return you already filed: income, deductions, credits or filing status.
  • You usually do not need to amend for a math error or a missing form the IRS can see. Wait for a notice or ask first.
  • In most cases you have three years from the filing date, or two years from payment, to claim a refund.
  • If you owe more, file and pay as soon as you can to limit interest and penalties.
  • State returns are amended separately, and you can track your federal amended return online.

Finding a mistake after you file is stressful, but it is more common than people think. A 1099 shows up late, a deduction was left off, or a dependent was claimed on the wrong return. The IRS has a process for it, and in most cases the fix is straightforward. Here is how I walk clients through the decision.

What Form 1040-X is for

Form 1040-X, Amended U.S. Individual Income Tax Return, corrects a return you have already filed. It has three columns: the amount as originally reported, the net change, and the corrected amount. You explain each change in Part II. You can use it to change your filing status, income, deductions or credits. The IRS says you can e-file Form 1040-X with tax filing software that supports it, or mail a paper form.

When you should amend

  • You left out income, such as a 1099-NEC, 1099-K or W-2 that arrived late.
  • You missed a deduction or credit you qualify for, such as education credits or business expenses.
  • Your filing status or dependents were wrong.
  • Your records changed, for example a corrected Form 1099 or a Schedule K-1 that was revised.

If you realize you underreported income, it is generally better to amend before the IRS contacts you about it. If you owe more tax, the IRS says to pay as soon as possible to limit interest and penalties.

When you usually do not need to

  • Math errors. The IRS corrects simple arithmetic mistakes and tells you.
  • Forgot to attach a form or schedule. The IRS commonly contacts you to request it. Wait for the letter, and see my guide on decoding an IRS notice if one arrives.
  • Your original refund has not arrived yet. The IRS generally asks that you wait for it before filing an amended return, so the two do not collide.

The time limits

To claim a refund, you generally must file Form 1040-X within three years of the date you filed the original return, or within two years of the date you paid the tax, whichever is later. A return filed before the due date is treated as filed on the due date. These are the general rules from the IRS instructions; special rules apply for things like bad debts and certain carrybacks. If you owe more tax, there is no benefit to waiting, because interest keeps running from the original due date.

How to file it

  1. Gather your original return and the documents behind the change.
  2. Complete the three columns. Show the original figures, the change and the corrected figures line by line.
  3. Explain the change in Part II in clear, short sentences.
  4. Attach any new or changed forms and schedules, such as a new Schedule C or Form 8863, but not forms that did not change.
  5. Sign and send it. E-file if your software supports it for that tax year, or mail it to the address in the Form 1040-X instructions. File a separate Form 1040-X for each tax year and mail each in its own envelope.
  6. Pay what you owe right away if there is a balance, rather than waiting for the IRS to bill you.

Business owners should be especially careful here. If your fix involves business expenses, make sure your books back it up. A change like a home office deduction needs real records, which I cover in the home office deduction guide for renters and homeowners.

How long does it take?

Amended returns are processed by hand more often than regular returns, so patience is required. The IRS instructions say to allow 8 to 12 weeks, and in some cases processing can take up to 16 weeks. You can follow the status with the IRS tool “Where’s My Amended Return?” It can take up to three weeks after you file for the return to show up. Do not file a second amended return for the same item while the first is pending.

What about your state return?

A change on your federal return often changes your state return. Missouri and Kansas each have their own amended return process and their own time limits, so check the instructions on the Missouri Department of Revenue and Kansas Department of Revenue websites. If you pay the Kansas City, Missouri earnings tax or file a local return, a change in income may affect that too. Because the IRS shares information with state tax agencies, it is better to report a change yourself than to wait for a state letter.

If the source document was wrong

Sometimes the original return was fine and the source document was wrong. If an employer, a payment app or a bank issued an incorrect form, ask the issuer for a corrected one. Amend only after you have the corrected document, so you only have to do it once.

The short version

If the change is real, the document is final and the time limit has not passed, file Form 1040-X, explain it plainly and pay any balance. If you are unsure whether it is worth it, ask a preparer to look before you file. You can learn more about how I work on my about page.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to file an amended return for a refund?

Generally three years from the date you filed the original return, or two years from the date you paid the tax, whichever is later. A return filed early is treated as filed on the due date.

Do I need to amend for a math error or a forgotten form?

Usually not. The IRS normally corrects math errors and writes to you if a form or schedule is missing. Wait for its letter before filing Form 1040-X.

How do I check the status of my amended return?

Use the IRS tool “Where’s My Amended Return?” on IRS.gov. The IRS says to allow 8 to 12 weeks, and in some cases processing can take up to 16 weeks, so give it time before calling. It can take up to three weeks for a new amended return to appear in the tool.
