Where Is My Tax Refund? How Long It Takes and Why It Gets Delayed

Refund timing is one of the most common questions from February to April. Here is what the IRS says to expect and how to check without calling in circles.

By Robert Allen Bautista, CPA candidate · Kansas City tax preparer and accountant · Published 2026-11-19

Key takeaways

  • The IRS says most e-filed refunds with direct deposit are issued in under 21 calendar days, but some take longer.
  • Use the IRS Where's My Refund tool for status. It needs your Social Security number, filing status and exact refund amount.
  • Refunds that include certain credits cannot be released before the date set by law, which is in mid-February.
  • Errors, missing information, identity checks and paper filing are the usual causes of delays.

You filed, you are waiting, and the bank account is still empty. Almost everyone checks the refund status more often than they will admit. Here is how the process works according to the IRS, what the status messages mean, and what to do if your refund is slow.

The timing the IRS gives

The IRS says most refunds are issued in less than 21 calendar days when you e-file and choose direct deposit, and that some take longer. That is a typical case, not a promise. Paper returns take longer because they are processed by hand. Choosing a paper check also adds mailing time. If you are filing for the first time or have never filed electronically before, build in extra patience and avoid planning a large payment around a specific date.

How to check your status

The IRS tool is called Where’s My Refund, and you can reach it on irs.gov or through the IRS2Go app. You will need three things: your Social Security number or ITIN, your filing status, and the exact refund amount shown on your return, in whole dollars. The IRS says the tool is updated once a day, usually overnight, so checking five times a day tells you nothing new. The tool shows one of three stages:

  • Return Received. The IRS has your return and is processing it.
  • Refund Approved. Your refund was approved and the IRS is preparing to send it.
  • Refund Sent. The IRS sent your refund to your bank, or mailed a check.

After “Refund Sent,” your bank may need a few more days to post the deposit, so allow a little time before you worry.

The credit rule that surprises people

By law, the IRS cannot issue refunds before mid-February if your return claims the Earned Income Tax Credit or the Additional Child Tax Credit. The IRS holds the entire refund, not only the credit part, until that date. If you file in January and see no movement, this is the first thing to check. The exact date and the dates the IRS expects most such refunds to arrive are published on irs.gov each season.

Common reasons a refund is late

  • A mistake or missing item. Typos in a Social Security number, a name that does not match records, or a missing form can stop a return.
  • Math or entry errors. The IRS can correct some of these automatically, which adds time, and sends a notice explaining the change.
  • Identity verification. The IRS may hold a return and send a letter asking you to confirm who you are. Follow the instructions on the letter and on irs.gov, not a link in a text or email.
  • Offsets. Your refund can be reduced to pay certain debts, such as past-due federal or state obligations, child support or student loans. You get a notice explaining it.
  • An amended return or review. If the return is selected for further review, the IRS will contact you by mail.
  • Paper filing or a banking problem. A closed account or a typo in your routing number can send a deposit back.

When to contact the IRS

The IRS says to contact it if the tool tells you to, or if it has been more than 21 days since you e-filed and there is no update. Before you do, collect your return and have your Social Security number ready. If a letter has already arrived, read the notice number in the corner before you call. My article on decoding an IRS notice explains how to tell what each letter is asking.

If you filed and your refund looks wrong

A refund that arrives smaller than expected usually means the IRS adjusted something or applied an offset. A notice should arrive by mail explaining the change. Compare it with your return before you do anything. If you made the mistake, an amended return may be the fix, and the process is covered in my guide to Form 1040-X. Do not file a second original return, because that can delay things further.

Your state refund works separately

State refunds are handled by the state, not the IRS. Missouri and Kansas each have their own refund status tools and timelines. Look at dor.mo.gov and ksrevenue.gov for the current instructions, because a federal refund that has arrived does not tell you anything about the state one.

What you can do before you file to avoid delays

  • Check each Social Security number and spelling against the cards.
  • E-file and choose direct deposit, and double check the routing and account numbers.
  • Make sure every income form is included, since a missing form can trigger a notice later.
  • If the IRS gave you an Identity Protection PIN, enter the current one.
  • Keep a copy of your return and any confirmation.

A calmer way to think about it

A refund is your own money coming back from over-withholding during the year. Planning a budget around a refund date is risky. If the arrival date matters, file as early as you have every document, use direct deposit and keep your confirmation. If you want less dependence on a refund in the first place, a paycheck checkup helps you set withholding closer to what you will owe.

Frequently asked questions

How long does an IRS refund take?

The IRS says most e-filed returns with direct deposit are issued in under 21 calendar days, though some take longer.

Why is my refund delayed?

Common causes include errors, missing information, identity verification, an offset for a debt, a paper return, or the mid-February hold on returns with certain credits.

How often does Where's My Refund update?

The IRS says it updates once per day, usually overnight.
