Estimated Taxes Month by Month: A Calendar for Your First Freelance Year in Kansas City

Nobody withholds tax from freelance income, so the IRS expects you to pay during the year. Here is a calendar you can follow from day one.

By Robert Allen Bautista · Kansas City tax preparer and accountant · Published 2026-11-15

Key takeaways

  • Federal estimated payments are generally due around April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15. Weekend and holiday dates move to the next business day.
  • The safest method is usually paying at least 100% of last year's total tax (110% if last year's adjusted gross income was above $150,000), spread across four payments.
  • A first-year freelancer who also had a W-2 job can count that withholding toward the total.
  • If you miss a payment, pay as soon as you can. The underpayment charge accrues over time, so a late payment beats no payment.
  • Missouri and Kansas have their own estimated tax rules, so check both states if they apply to you.

The first year of freelancing hides a trap. You do not have a paycheck stub telling you what you owe, and a tax bill arrives only after the year is over. By then the money is spent. The fix is to treat taxes like a bill you pay four times a year. Below is the calendar I give new clients, built around the common case of someone starting to freelance and living in the Kansas City area.

The four deadlines and what they cover

Estimated tax is paid in four installments, but the periods are not equal quarters. Roughly:

  • Payment 1, around April 15: income from January 1 through March 31.
  • Payment 2, around June 15: income from April 1 through May 31.
  • Payment 3, around September 15: income from June 1 through August 31.
  • Payment 4, around January 15 of the next year: income from September 1 through December 31.

When a date lands on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day. Confirm the current year's dates on IRS.gov.

How to figure the amount without guessing

There are two practical ways. The first is the prior-year safe harbor. Look at the total tax line on last year's Form 1040. If your payments and withholding for this year add up to at least 100% of that figure, you generally avoid the underpayment penalty, even if your income grew. If last year's adjusted gross income was above $150,000 (or $75,000 if married filing separately), the threshold is 110%. Divide by four and you have a payment that does not need to be recalculated.

The second is the current-year method: pay at least 90% of this year's tax. That requires a decent forecast of income, which a new freelancer rarely has, so the prior-year number is a better anchor in year one. If you had very little tax last year because you were in school or between jobs, the safe harbor can be very small, and you may still want to set aside more to avoid a large April bill. The penalty rules protect you from interest and charges, not from the tax itself.

There is also a small exception: if you expect to owe less than $1,000 after withholding and credits, no estimated payments are required.

The month-by-month calendar

January and February

If you freelanced late last year, your final payment for that year is due in mid-January. Open a separate savings account and name it taxes. Set up your books, which is where bookkeeping software or a bookkeeper helps. Gather any 1099 forms, and if you sell on a platform, read my first 1099-K guide to see how those forms relate to what you report.

March

Total your income for the first quarter. Move 25% to 30% of net profit into the tax account as a starting rule of thumb, adjusting once you see real figures.

April

Prior-year return due and payment 1 due. If you are filing an extension, remember that it extends the time to file, not the time to pay.

May

Close out April and May income and expenses. Compare to the first estimate. If profit is running far above the prior year, increase the next payment.

June

Payment 2 due mid-month. Pay from the tax savings account, not from your operating account, so the money is simply moved.

July and August

Quiet months. Use them to reconcile mileage logs, receipts and software subscriptions. A mid-year review is the cheapest time to catch a mistake.

September

Payment 3 due mid-month, covering June through August.

October and November

Project year-end profit. If it will be unusually high or low, adjust the final payment. This is also the window for year-end moves such as equipment purchases or retirement plan contributions.

December

Last chance for expense timing. Estimate the January payment.

What to do if you missed a payment

Do not wait for the next deadline. Pay what you can now through IRS Direct Pay or your IRS online account and note the date. The underpayment charge works like interest, calculated by period, so paying late reduces the charge compared with paying nothing. You can also increase the next payment, or, if you have a W-2 job, raise your withholding on Form W-4. Withholding is treated as paid evenly through the year, which can cover earlier shortfalls. When you file, Form 2210 calculates any charge, and the annualized income method can reduce it if your income was concentrated late in the year.

Do not forget the states and the city

Missouri and Kansas both have estimated tax rules for residents with income not subject to withholding, and the thresholds and forms differ. Frequently asked questions

Do I have to make estimated payments in my first year?

Generally yes if you expect to owe $1,000 or more after withholding and credits. If you owed no tax last year as a full-year resident, you may have no penalty exposure, but you still owe the tax when you file.

How much should I set aside from each payment I receive?

A common starting range is 25% to 30% of net profit for federal tax, self-employment tax and state tax combined. Adjust it after you see your actual numbers, and expect it to differ for higher or lower incomes.

How do I pay estimated taxes?

You can pay through IRS Direct Pay, your IRS online account, or by mail with a voucher from Form 1040-ES. Keep the confirmation for every payment.

What if my income is uneven during the year?

The annualized income method on Form 2210 can lower or remove the penalty when income is concentrated in later quarters. It takes more recordkeeping, so keep monthly profit totals.

Is the safe harbor the same for the states?

Not necessarily. Missouri and Kansas have their own rules, forms and thresholds. Check each state's revenue department site for current requirements.
