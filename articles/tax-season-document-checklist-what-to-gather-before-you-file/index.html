Tax Season Document Checklist: What to Gather Before You File

Most filing delays are not about the tax law. They are about a missing form. Here is what to gather, and when each piece usually shows up.

By Robert Allen Bautista, CPA candidate · Kansas City tax preparer and accountant · Published 2026-11-18

Key takeaways

  • Start a folder now and add each form as it arrives; most arrive in January.
  • Employers generally must furnish Form W-2 to employees by January 31, and most Forms 1099 follow the same date.
  • Wait for every document before you file, because a late or corrected form can mean an amended return.
  • The IRS generally says to keep records at least three years from filing.

Every year somebody files in a rush, then a form arrives in the mail two weeks later. That is how a clean return turns into an amended one. The fix is boring and works: build a checklist before tax season starts, add to it as paperwork arrives, and file only when it is complete. Below is the list I use as a Kansas City tax preparer, organized by where the paperwork comes from.

Income documents

  • Form W-2 from each employer. The IRS says employers generally must send these to employees by January 31. If you changed jobs, expect more than one. I walk through each box in how to read your W-2, box by box.
  • Form 1099-NEC or 1099-MISC from clients who paid you as a freelancer or contractor. These are also generally due to recipients by January 31.
  • Form 1099-K if a payment app or marketplace reported your sales. If you sell on Etsy, eBay or Facebook Marketplace, this guide to your first 1099-K explains how to handle it.
  • Form 1099-INT and 1099-DIV from banks and brokerages for interest and dividends.
  • Form 1099-B or a consolidated brokerage statement if you sold stock or other investments. Some brokerage statements arrive later than January 31, so do not be surprised if these come in February.
  • Form 1099-G if you received unemployment compensation or a state refund, and Form 1099-R for retirement distributions.
  • Form SSA-1099 if you received Social Security benefits.

Deduction and credit documents

  • Form 1098 for mortgage interest, and your property tax records if you plan to itemize.
  • Form 1098-T for tuition and Form 1098-E for student loan interest.
  • Form 1095-A if you bought health coverage through the Health Insurance Marketplace. The IRS says you need it to reconcile the premium tax credit on Form 8962, so do not file without it.
  • Childcare provider details. To claim the child and dependent care credit you generally must report the provider’s name, address and taxpayer identification number on Form 2441, so ask your provider early.
  • Charitable receipts for donations, especially anything large or non-cash.
  • Self-employed expense records: mileage, receipts, software and supplies. If you work from home, see the home office records guide.

Personal and filing information

  • Photo ID and Social Security numbers (or ITINs) for you, your spouse and any dependents, spelled exactly as on the cards.
  • Last year’s federal and state returns. They help with carryovers and confirm names, dependents and filing status.
  • Bank routing and account numbers for direct deposit. The IRS recommends direct deposit with e-file as the fastest way to get a refund.
  • Any IRS or state letters you received during the year.
  • Your Identity Protection PIN, if the IRS gave you one. A return can be rejected without it.

What if a form never arrives?

First contact the payer, whether that is an employer, bank or client. If a W-2 is still missing by late February, the IRS describes steps for taxpayers who cannot get one, including estimating wages and filing Form 4852 as a substitute. That is a last resort, because estimates can differ from what the employer actually reported. If a form arrives with the wrong amount or name, ask the payer for a corrected version before you file.

Why waiting for every form matters

The IRS receives copies of most of these forms from the payers. If your return leaves one out, the mismatch can trigger a notice later. If you file early and a corrected form arrives, you may have to amend; see when and how to file a 1040-X. A week of patience is cheaper than a months-long fix.

State paperwork

Missouri and Kansas returns generally build on your federal numbers, so the same documents apply. The state revenue departments publish their own forms and instructions at dor.mo.gov and ksrevenue.gov; check them for your filing year, especially if you moved or worked in both states during the year.

How long to keep everything

The IRS says to keep records at least three years from the date you filed in most cases, and longer in some situations. A scanned copy in a folder you can find beats a perfect filing system you never use.

A simple routine

  1. Create a folder, paper or digital, labeled with the tax year.
  2. Each time a form arrives, drop it in and check it off the list above.
  3. In early February, compare your folder to last year’s return to spot anything missing.
  4. Only then send everything to your preparer or open your software.

Common mistakes

The first is filing from a pay stub instead of the W-2. The pay stub shows year-to-date figures before some adjustments, and the W-2 is the document the IRS matches against. The second is forgetting a small job or a short-term gig, because the form for it went to an old address. If you moved during the year, check your mail at the old address and update your address with each payer. The third is assuming a small amount of interest or a modest side payment does not need to be reported. Reportable income is reportable even when no form arrives, and the IRS says you are responsible for including all of it.

A fourth, quieter mistake is treating the checklist as a one-time task. If you keep the folder going all year, adding receipts and statements monthly, January becomes an assembly job instead of a search. That is especially true for freelancers and anyone with rental or business income.

Frequently asked questions

When do employers have to send W-2s?

The IRS says employers generally must furnish Form W-2 to employees by January 31.

What if I never get a W-2?

Contact the employer first. If it is still missing by late February, the IRS describes steps for taxpayers who cannot get one, including Form 4852 as a substitute, which should be a last resort.

How long should I keep tax records?

The IRS generally says to keep records at least three years from the date you filed, and longer in certain situations.
