Freelancer Planning for 2027: Estimated Tax Dates and Safe Harbors

Mark four dates in your calendar now. One of them is not the date you would guess.

By Robert Allen Bautista · Kansas City tax educator · Published 2026-11-25

Key takeaways

  • The four federal estimated tax due dates for 2027 are April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2027, and January 18, 2028.
  • The January date moves because January 15, 2028 is a Saturday and Monday, January 17, is a federal holiday.
  • You can generally avoid the underpayment penalty if you owe under $1,000, or pay at least 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's, whichever is smaller.
  • Kansas City, Missouri has a 1% earnings tax on net profit that no one withholds for you, so build it into your set-aside.

If you work for yourself, nobody takes tax out of your pay. The IRS expects you to send it in four payments during the year, and missing them can cost you a penalty even if you pay the full bill in April. This article gives the 2027 dates, the rule that protects you from a penalty and a Kansas City reminder.

The 2027 due dates

For estimated tax purposes, the year is divided into four payment periods, and the usual due dates are April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15 of the following year. When a date lands on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, the IRS says the payment is on time if you make it on the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday or holiday. Here is how that plays out for 2027.

  • First payment: Thursday, April 15, 2027.
  • Second payment: Tuesday, June 15, 2027.
  • Third payment: Wednesday, September 15, 2027.
  • Fourth payment: Tuesday, January 18, 2028. January 15, 2028 is a Saturday, and Monday, January 17, 2028 is Martin Luther King Jr. Day, so the next business day is Tuesday.

I worked out each weekday from the calendar. The IRS may publish its own 2027 schedule later, so confirm the dates on IRS.gov before you pay.

Two earlier dates matter too. Your fourth payment for 2026 is due Friday, January 15, 2027, and your 2026 return and any balance are due Thursday, April 15, 2027. That makes April 15, 2027 a double date: the 2026 return and the first 2027 payment.

The safe harbor rule

A safe harbor is a set of conditions that protects you from the penalty. The IRS says most taxpayers will avoid the underpayment penalty if they owe less than $1,000 in tax after subtracting their withholding and credits, or if they paid at least 90% of the tax for the current year, or 100% of the tax shown on the return for the prior year, whichever is smaller.

Higher earners get a stricter rule. IRS Publication 505 says that if your adjusted gross income for the prior year was more than $150,000 ($75,000 if you will file as married filing separately), you substitute 110% for 100%. That wording comes from the 2026 edition of the publication. The 2027 edition is not out yet, so confirm the thresholds before relying on them.

A fictional example

This is a made-up example. A freelance designer in Kansas City had $12,000 of total tax on her 2026 return and an adjusted gross income well under $150,000. In 2027, her income jumps and she cannot tell what she will owe.

The 100% of last year's tax rule gives her a target she can compute today. She divides $12,000 by four and pays $3,000 each quarter. If she ends up owing more at filing time, she still owes the extra tax, but she generally should not face an underpayment penalty for that gap. If her income drops instead, the 90% rule for the current year may give her a lower target. Which rule helps most depends on how the year goes.

How to calculate each payment

  1. Estimate your net profit for the year: income minus business expenses.
  2. Estimate your self-employment tax and income tax. My article on why your first freelance tax bill was so big shows the pieces.
  3. Subtract any withholding from a job.
  4. Divide by four, or use the prior-year safe harbor number.
  5. Pay through your IRS online account or another method the IRS accepts, and keep the confirmation.

The Kansas City angle

Kansas City, Missouri has a 1% earnings tax. For self-employed people it generally applies to net profit, and no employer withholds it. That makes it easy to leave out of your set-aside, even though it is smaller than your federal and state tax. If you live in the city or do the work there, add 1% of your expected net profit to your plan. My article on Kansas City earnings tax scenarios covers residents, non-residents and people who split their week.

The city's filing and payment schedule can differ from the federal one, and I did not verify a 2027 city calendar here, so check with the Kansas City, Missouri Revenue Division. Missouri and Kansas estimated tax rules have their own dates too, so look those up before you pay.

Frequently asked questions

When are estimated taxes due in 2027?

The usual federal dates fall on Thursday, April 15, Tuesday, June 15 and Wednesday, September 15, 2027. The fourth payment is due Tuesday, January 18, 2028, because January 15, 2028 is a Saturday and January 17 is a federal holiday.

How do I avoid an underpayment penalty?

The IRS says most taxpayers avoid it if they owe less than $1,000 after withholding and credits, or if they paid at least 90% of the current year's tax or 100% of the prior year's tax, whichever is smaller. Higher-income taxpayers substitute 110% for 100%.

Does the Kansas City earnings tax need estimated payments?

Kansas City, Missouri has a 1% earnings tax that generally applies to net profit for self-employed people, and no one withholds it for you. Check the city's Revenue Division for current forms and deadlines.
