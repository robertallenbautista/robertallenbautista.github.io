Filing an Extension in 2027: Form 4868, Form 7004 and Why Payment Is Still Due

An extension gives you more time to file. The tax is still due on the original date.

By Robert Allen Bautista · Kansas City tax educator · Published 2026-11-26

Key takeaways

  • Form 7004 for a calendar-year partnership or S corporation return is due Monday, March 15, 2027. Form 4868 and payment for an individual return are due Thursday, April 15, 2027.
  • The extended due date for an individual return is Friday, October 15, 2027.
  • The IRS says an extension of time to file is not an extension of time to pay. Interest still applies to tax not paid by the original due date.
  • You do not need a finished return to pay with an extension. You can estimate what you owe from withholding, income records and last year's return.

Every spring, some people file for an extension and assume they have bought time to pay. An extension gives you more time to file your return, and the tax is still due on the original date.

This article covers the two forms most readers will meet, Form 4868 for individuals and Form 7004 for many business returns, plus the dates for the 2026 tax year.

The dates for the 2026 return

Check the weekday for each date, since weekends and holidays move deadlines.

  • Form 7004 for a calendar-year partnership or S corporation return: Monday, March 15, 2027.
  • Form 4868 and payment for an individual return: Thursday, April 15, 2027.
  • Extended due date for an individual return: Friday, October 15, 2027.

For a calendar-year partnership or S corporation, the return and Form 7004 are both due March 15, the 15th day of the 3rd month after the tax year ends. The extension is generally six months, which moves the filing deadline to September 15. A calendar-year C corporation is on a different schedule: its return is due April 15, and an extension moves it to October 15.

What Form 4868 does

Form 4868 is the automatic extension request for individuals. The IRS says you can get until October 15 to file your return. IRS Topic 304 adds that you must request the extension by your return's due date to avoid the late-filing penalty.

You can file the form on paper, by e-file through an IRS partner or a tax professional, or through IRS Free File. You can also get an extension by paying online and checking the extension box when you pay, with no separate form. The IRS says that if you e-file, you will need your prior year's adjusted gross income, and you will get an electronic acknowledgment.

Some taxpayers living abroad get an automatic two-month extension. If that is you, ask your preparer about the extra time and what it does and does not change.

What the extension does not do

An extension of time to file is not an extension of time to pay. The IRS page for Form 4868 says you must pay what you owe by the April filing due date, and that the extension is only for filing your return. Interest still applies to tax not paid by the original due date.

For the business side, the instructions for Form 7004 say the form does not extend the time to pay any tax due. Tax not paid by the original March 15 due date accrues interest from that date, and a late-payment penalty of one-half of one percent per month may apply. That penalty language comes from the instructions for the business form, so ask your preparer how it applies to your own return.

In practice, if you owe, an extension does not save you money. It can spare you the late-filing penalty and a rushed return.

How to figure out what to pay

You do not need a finished return to make a payment with an extension. You can estimate. Follow these steps.

  1. Gather what you have: pay stubs, withholding so far, income records and your last year's return.
  2. Make a rough tax calculation. Many preparers will do a quick estimate for you.
  3. Subtract what you already paid through withholding and estimated payments.
  4. Pay the balance by April 15, 2027.

If you are a little off, you will pay the difference when you file. If you overpay, the extra is refunded or applied to next year.

How to pay

The IRS says to pay electronically when you can. Options in IRS Topic 304 include Direct Pay, your IRS online account, a debit or credit card, or a digital wallet. You will get a confirmation number, so keep it with your records. If you pay by card, there is usually a processing fee, so compare costs.

Why people extend

People extend for a range of reasons.

  • A tax form that is late or wrong, like a K-1 from a partnership or an S corporation.
  • A year with a lot of changes, such as a business sale, a move or a death in the family.
  • Records you need to rebuild.
  • A new tax rule, like the new tips and overtime deductions, where a corrected W-2 may be coming.
  • A business return that needs to be finished before the owner's personal return.

Some people extend because they are not ready and do not want to rush, which is a fine reason. Pay what you expect to owe on time.

A made-up example

A freelancer in the Kansas City area has a profit that is hard to pin down because some clients have not sent final paperwork. In early April she makes a rough estimate and figures she owes about $4,000. She files Form 4868 and pays $4,000 by April 15. In September she completes the return and finds the actual tax is $4,300. She pays the $300 difference when she files, with interest on that part. Had she skipped the payment in April, she would have owed interest and a late-payment penalty on the entire amount. These numbers are invented to show how the steps work.

S corporations and partnerships

For entity returns, the Form 7004 deadline falls a month before the individual deadline, March 15 versus April 15. Owners need the entity's numbers for their own returns. If you own a share of an S corporation or partnership and the entity extends, your own return may need an extension too, because your K-1 comes from the entity return. Partnerships and S corporations that qualify may be able to request additional time under a separate regulation, and the instructions for Form 7004 describe that option. Ask your preparer if that applies.

Form 7004 covers business returns only. Your personal return needs its own extension on Form 4868.

Questions to ask your preparer

  • Should I file an extension, or can I finish my return before the deadline?
  • How much should I pay with my extension request?
  • If my S corporation or partnership files an extension, do I need to file one too?
  • What happens to interest if I pay more or less than I owe?
  • Will an extension change when I get my refund, if I am owed one?

Frequently asked questions

When is Form 4868 due for the 2026 tax year?

Form 4868 and payment for an individual return are due Thursday, April 15, 2027. The IRS says the extension gives you until October 15 to file, which is Friday, October 15, 2027.

Does an extension give me more time to pay?

No. The IRS says you must pay what you owe by the April filing due date and that the extension is only for filing your return. Interest still applies to tax not paid by the original due date.

Does Form 7004 cover my personal return?

No. Form 7004 covers business returns only. Your personal return needs its own extension on Form 4868.
