Form 1099-K Thresholds: A Plain-English Timeline of What Changed

The 1099-K threshold has been announced, delayed, phased in and then reversed. Here is the timeline from the IRS pages, and what stays true no matter which number applies.

By Robert Allen Bautista · Kansas City tax educator, CPA candidate · Published 2026-11-20

Key takeaways

  • Per the IRS, a payment app or online marketplace must report when gross payments for goods or services exceed $20,000 and there are more than 200 transactions.
  • A 2021 law lowered the threshold to $600, the IRS delayed it, and a later law retroactively reinstated the earlier threshold.
  • A platform may still send you a form for less than the threshold, and some states set lower limits.
  • The threshold decides who gets a form. It never decides what is taxable.
  • For your year, confirm the number on IRS.gov instead of trusting a headline.

$600, $5,000, $2,500, $20,000: you have probably heard all four, and per the IRS the current rule is more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions. Each number was real at some point in the news, and that is exactly why people are confused about whether they should expect a form. This article is a timeline. It is not a how-to for your return, which I cover in other guides. It is a way to make sense of the headlines using what the IRS itself says.

First, what the threshold actually controls

Form 1099-K reports payments you received through a payment card or a third party network, such as a marketplace or payment app. The reporting threshold controls when the company has to send you and the IRS a form. It does not control whether your income is taxable. The IRS says plainly, on its page Understanding your Form 1099-K, that you must report income from selling goods or services whether or not you receive a form. Keep that in mind through everything below.

Step one: the older rule

The IRS Taxpayer Advocate Service describes the earlier rule in its blog post Is This the Year You Finally Get a Form 1099-K? For third party networks, a form was required only when a seller had more than $20,000 in receipts and more than 200 transactions. A seller with plenty of small sales and less than $20,000 would usually never see a form from the platform.

Step two: the 2021 law

The same Taxpayer Advocate post explains that legislation in 2021 lowered the threshold from $20,000 to $600. It also dropped the idea of a minimum transaction count. The post estimates this would have added roughly 30 million forms per year. That is the change most sellers remember, because the number sounded so small.

Step three: the delays and the planned phase-in

The $600 rule did not take effect right away. According to the Taxpayer Advocate, the IRS delayed it for two years and then announced a gradual phase-in. The same post lists $5,000 for 2024 and $2,500 for 2025, with no minimum transaction count, and $600 after 2025. If you filed in the last couple of seasons and received a form for a few thousand dollars, this is the stretch of time you were living in.

Step four: the reinstated threshold

In 2025 Congress passed the One, Big, Beautiful Bill Act. The IRS page Form 1099-K FAQs: General information says the law retroactively reinstated the reporting threshold in effect before the 2021 law. In plain terms, the IRS now states that payment apps and online marketplaces must report when total gross payments for goods or services exceed $20,000 and there are more than 200 transactions for a payee.

Two details from that same FAQ matter. First, payment card transactions have no threshold at all, so a card reader or merchant account can still generate a form from the first dollar. Second, a third party network may still send a form for amounts lower than the thresholds, and some states set lower thresholds of their own. So “below $20,000, no form” is a general rule, not a promise.

What the timeline means for you in practice

Scenario one: you sold a lot, once

For example, imagine a hypothetical seller who cleared out a storage unit and moved a few dozen items for a total of several thousand dollars. Under the reinstated rule, that person may never receive a form. The sales are still reportable if any were taxable, such as an item sold for more than its cost. The missing form changes nothing about the duty to report a gain.

Scenario two: you run a steady small shop

Now imagine a hypothetical craft seller with hundreds of small orders and well under $20,000 in total. The platform may or may not send a form, and the amount on it, if any, is gross. Her income is taxable on a profit basis either way, and she should be keeping records from the first order. My guide to your first 1099-K covers what to do with the form when it arrives.

Scenario three: you took cards at a market booth

A hypothetical vendor using a card reader at weekend markets should expect that payment card reporting has no threshold, so a form is likely. It will show the gross card payments for the year, before fees and refunds.

Why the number on the form is never your income

The IRS says in the same FAQ that the gross amount on a form does not account for your original purchase price, called basis, and does not show whether items were sold at a gain or a loss. It also does not reflect fees, refunds or shipping. So whichever threshold applied in a given year, the form is a starting point. My article on your first 1099-K as an Etsy, eBay or Marketplace seller walks through how to get from that gross figure to a profit figure.

How to check what applies to your year

  1. Identify the tax year. The rules were written differently for different years, and the retroactive change means older coverage can be out of date.
  2. Read the IRS pages above on IRS.gov, which are updated when the law or guidance changes.
  3. Look at the form you received. If a platform sent one, it is in the IRS system even if you think it should not have been.
  4. Check your state. Some states set their own lower thresholds, so check with the Missouri Department of Revenue or the Kansas Department of Revenue if you live in the Kansas City area.
  5. Keep your own records either way. They are your protection if a form is wrong, late or never arrives.

The short version

The threshold went from $20,000 and 200 transactions, to $600 on paper, to a delay and a planned phase-in, and back to $20,000 and 200 transactions under the law the IRS says was retroactively reinstated. Either way, your income is taxable on a net basis, and the record-keeping you do today is what makes a confusing form manageable. Frequently asked questions

What is the Form 1099-K threshold right now?

Per the IRS, a payment app or online marketplace must report when total gross payments for goods or services exceed $20,000 and there are more than 200 transactions for a payee. Check IRS.gov for your specific tax year.

Can I get a 1099-K even if I am under the threshold?

Yes. The IRS says a third party network may still send a form for lower amounts, payment card transactions have no threshold, and some states set lower thresholds.

If I do not get a 1099-K, do I still report my income?

Yes. The IRS says income from selling goods or services must be reported whether or not you receive a form.
